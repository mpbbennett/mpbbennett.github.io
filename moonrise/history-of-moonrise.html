History of Moonrise

The history of Moonrise spans over two thousand years of recorded events, from the rise of the High Elven Empire to the present day. This page presents a master timeline and detailed accounts of each major era.

The oldest living humans remember the late Clan Wars. The oldest dwarves remember the Giant Overlordship. The oldest elves were born during the era of the Arcane Order. The oldest dragons were alive during the Goblin Despotate.

Prehistory

Little is known of the earliest history of Moonrise. Elves and gnomes are believed to have migrated from the Feywild. Dragons came from another continent. Dwarves, Goblins, Halflings, and Orcs have ancient roots in the land. Humans are found everywhere but their ultimate origins are unclear.

The High Elven Empire (~2,400–1,290 years ago)

A classical dynastic empire ruled by high elves over many subject peoples, more or less integrated. At its height it dominated the entire region. The Empire fought wars against the Drow, a subterranean elven people.

During this era, the Wayfinder tradition was founded (~2,000 years ago), and the King of Roses became the first High King of the wood elves, establishing what would eventually become the Forest Kingdoms (~1,500 years ago).

The Empire was destroyed in a natural cataclysm roughly 1,290 years ago, the precise nature of which is now obscure. The Drow are somehow connected to the Cataclysm. In its wake, high elves were scattered across the continent. Many flocked to the newly founded Congregation — the first Prophet emerged roughly 950 years ago — to atone for the violence and hubris of their shattered empire.

The Intermediate Period (~1,290–750 years ago)

The period between the Cataclysm and the rise of the Goblin Despotate was one of fragmentation. Among the notable figures of this era:

  • A halfling warlord known as Mete Half-Ear established the largest empire of the period.
  • The Greenspire Dwarves and the wood Elves fought the War of the Beard, beginning as a local feud over tree-cutting and escalating into a great war. It ended in stalemate, leaving deep cultural mistrust.
  • The Necromancer (the Necromancer Lord of Hollowmere) established a sizeable empire in the southeast, exploiting the great number of recent dead left by the Ashen Crusade’s predecessor conflicts. Based in the city of Hollowmere, they ruled most of southeast Moonrise before being defeated by the Arcane Order. The modern capital of the Hegemon’s domain was built atop the ruins of the Necromancer’s capital.

The Goblin Despotate (~850–545 years ago)

In their golden age, goblin civilisation was concentrated in a collection of underground city-states, famous for technological innovation — including the construction of automata. One ruling house developed large-scale military automata and launched a period of imperial expansion, eventually creating the largest empire in the known world.

The Despotate’s need for scarce construction materials drove it to expand ever further, including a long and brutal campaign to conquer the wood elf forests. The hulking automata were poorly suited for guerrilla warfare, and the goblins responded by burning great tracts of forest, creating a cycle of escalating brutality.

The Ashen Crusade (~560–545 years ago)

The Despotate’s demands on its subject peoples — heavy taxation and military drafts — provoked a great mutiny. A conscript army killed its goblin commanders and joined the wood elves. Under a charismatic half-elf leader who claimed divine mandate from the Strong One at a volcanic temple, the rebellion became a crusade to eradicate goblinkind. The leader bathed in goblin blood and covered himself in ash, vowing not to wash until his work was done.

The Ashen Crusade broke the Despotate and then turned genocidal. The leader was eventually assassinated by his own confidants, sickened by his fanaticism, possibly with the connivance of emerging rulers. Long-standing rumours persist that the Despotate was never truly destroyed and that goblins still control kingdoms from behind the scenes.

The Arcane Order (~550–160 years ago)

A magocratic, mostly human empire that arose from the chaos following the Ashen Crusade. Also known in historical texts as the Imperium Arcanum or the Arcane Sovereignty.

The Arcane Order coalesced from an alliance between a magocratic city-state, a wizarding order, and a number of independent archmages. This coalition first tried to secure better government by elevating kings — the first proved ineffective, the second tyrannical. The mages determined they would have to rule themselves and created the Sovereignty. The archmagi proved no less ruthless when it came to disposing of their own Sovereigns who proved ineffective or tyrannical, and many Sovereigns came to unfortunate magical accidents.

The ruling elite were magic users organised in a ranked hierarchy: the Archmage-Sovereign at the top, then Archmagi (three subranks), Magi (twelve subranks), and Adepts (four subranks). Below them were the “clods” — the 90% of the population without magical abilities. Ranks entitled their holders to wear distinctive patterned robes.

Advancement was through a system of state-administered examinations covering wizardry, history, philosophy, and statecraft. The system was largely impartial, though corruption scandals were known. In practice, the Arcane Order was more collegial than despotic — Sovereigns who stood against the united opposition of the archmagi were swiftly deposed.

The system created tensions with the feudal nobility, who rankled at obeying the commands of their social inferiors. The common people generally favoured the competent governance the system produced.

Notable Sovereigns

  • Caradoc the Pious attempted to impose the Congregation as a state religion. Although ultimately unsuccessful, the Congregation grew significantly.
  • Gethin the Callous purchased the orc race as slaves from the Giants to work on great engineering projects. Notorious for brutality and paranoia, he was eventually assassinated by a member of the Veil, possibly with the connivance of some archmagi.
  • Cadfael the Lame was the most powerful wizard of the age, elevated reluctantly to the Sovereignty after the Callous’s assassination. They had lacked use of their legs from birth and moved using levitation magic. When every humanoid in Moonrise experienced a simultaneous nightmare (the Night of Anguish), the Lame abdicated, set sail alone for the Eastern Isles, and was never seen again. They are now remembered as an exemplary figure — powerful yet reluctant to rule, willing to relinquish power in service to their people.

Notable archmagi

  • Arianell, a Grandmaster of the Order, was condemned to elemental cessation around 250 years ago after publishing memoranda alleging a Faerie–Giant conspiracy against the Order and engaging Giant mercenaries on his own account. Subsequent Giant invasion under Sky-of-Rain has fed a minor rehabilitation literature.

The Capital

The capital of the Arcane Order was a flying city that could be moved around the empire as required. It was later yoked to the cloud giant castle of Sky-of-Rain. During the Clan Wars, rebels downed the entire city — a catastrophe causing massive loss of life. It still lies in ruins in an inaccessible location.

Formation of Key Factions

During the Arcane Order’s long reign, several major factions formed:
- The Combine (~300 years ago) — the dwarven trading cartel
- The Kin (~400 years ago) — the secret lycanthrope society

The Age of Giants (~220–90 years ago)

The westward migration of giants began in dribs and drabs almost a century before the Arcane Order’s fall. The Arcane Order attempted to manage the arrivals by integrating them into the military and relocating them across the continent. By the time Sky-of-Rain arrived with the last wave, nearly three-quarters of the Arcane Order’s non-magical forces were giants.

The Fall of the Arcane Order (~160 years ago)

Sky-of-Rain declared her intent to conquer, and most giants serving in the Arcane Order’s armies mutinied to join her. The downfall came remarkably quickly, aided by:

  1. The mass defection of giant soldiers
  2. Nobles who supported the invasion, hoping for better standing under a warrior-aristocratic regime
  3. The Bleeding King seizing the opportunity to claim the Lakeland
  4. A great slave revolt among the Orcs of the Isthmus

The archmagi, more pragmatic than heroic, surrendered relatively quickly to prevent further loss of life.

The Giant Overlordship and Clan Wars (~160–90 years ago)

Sky-of-Rain united the giant clans under her rule, but her death triggered the devastating Clan Wars. The conflict began when the Bleeding King whispered treacherous counsel to a Cloud giant clan leader, who orchestrated a betrayal that got the giant garrison out of the Lakeland — allowing the Bleeding King to seize it. The Cloud caste’s credibility never recovered from this spectacular treachery, and other castes began to challenge their dominance.

The Clan Wars lasted roughly 50 years before the giants exhausted themselves, allowing other peoples to reclaim their independence.

The Current Period (~100 years ago to present)

Formation of the League (~100 years ago)

Out of the chaos of the Clan Wars, The League was formed — a loose alliance of mostly human territories bound by a magical Compact. The League’s formation marks the epoch of the current dating system (years are counted “AC” — After the Compact).

The Bleeding King

The Bleeding King was an infamous vampire lord who ruled the Lakeland for roughly a century. Unlike most vampires, he was liberal in sharing his blood to create a vampiric aristocracy, providing both enforcers and a perverse form of social mobility. His classical depiction shows him offering his bleeding palms to two kneeling figures — this image appeared on his coinage.

The Coming of the Three (~5 years ago)

The Three appeared seemingly from nowhere, manifesting as beings of immense power claiming to be living gods. They defeated the Bleeding King and conquered the Lakeland, establishing a new empire and a rapidly growing religious cult.

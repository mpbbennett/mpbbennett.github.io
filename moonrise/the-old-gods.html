The Old Gods

The Old Gods refer to a loose and varied set of polytheistic beliefs held across Moonrise. Most worship is druidic and localised, but seven gods known as the Sacred Seven are worshipped more widely. They are usually depicted as androgynous humanoids with animal heads.

Earlier traditions knew ten Lunar Gods rather than seven. The three no longer counted among the Sacred Seven are the Sorrowful (wolf), the Mirthful (dolphin), and the Kind (cow). The Sorrowful is understood today as a persona of the Wise One rather than a separate god (see below). The Mirthful and the Kind survive as local gods in some regions; whether they too are aspects of one of the Seven, or independent figures, is disputed.

Aspects of the Wise One

The Wise One is held to manifest under several personae, each with its own cult, iconography, and tutelary domains. The standard name across Moonrise is the Wise One (or simply the Wise); the other personae are not generally treated as separate gods, though their cults can run with considerable independence.

  • The Wise One — the counsel-aspect, with the turtle, governing leadership, scholarship in old age, animal husbandry, and the slow virtues. The most widely worshipped persona.
  • The Sorrowful — the grief-aspect, with the wolf, governing mourning, memory of the dead, and the past. The Dustlistener Oracle of the Sorrowful is the surviving great cult-site (see below).
  • The Mournful Sea — the sea-and-dreams aspect, governing death by water, dreams, and madness. Principally venerated at the Temple of the Mournful Sea in Arrowmoor, where the cult holds the Mournful Sea to be the deepest of the three faces.

Old Gods doctrine in most parts of Moonrise treats the three as ranked under the standard name, with the counsel-aspect first; the inversions practised at Graveport, and the Strange-cult readings (below), are heterodox.

Local Gods

While the Sacred Seven are found in most cultures, they are often supplemented with local gods. These are understood either as additional members of the pantheon or as place-specific spirits (of a mountain, a river, etc.) whose status is different though not necessarily lesser.

A classic example is the cult of a river god in one League city, with its famous Floating Temple — a wooden temple complex floating in the middle of the river, anchored with magically strengthened chains, requiring continual rebuilding as the river wears it away.

Mystery Cults

The last century has seen the growth of mystery cults focused on one particular god. These are secretive, gradually initiating members into deeper levels of hidden knowledge through rituals usually connected to themes of death and rebirth.

  • The cult of the Strange reinterprets the Sorrowful persona of the Wise One, claiming the orthodox priesthood misunderstands its true nature
  • The cult of the Time-walker similarly reinterprets the Swift

Funerary Rituals

The most common funerary ritual involves cremation followed by scattering the ashes on water. The Congregation also practises cremation, though ashes are scattered in a garden or field (symbolising rebirth) rather than in water. Among cremation cultures, uncremated dead are sinister, associated with war, murder, and necromancy. Civilised warfare standardly includes allowing the loser to recover their dead for cremation — refusing is the ultimate disrespect.

Cremation is not universal:
- Dwarves lay their dead to rest beneath the bones of the earth
- Wood Elves bury their dead in sacred groves
- In the Old Dominion, the dead are interred in family mausoleums
- Wayfinders place their dead in “spires of silence” — high places for dismemberment by carrion birds

The Afterlife

The most common belief is that the dead are taken to the underworld by the Wise One — a grey, dark place of limbo and waiting. The minority of souls who excelled in the virtues of one of the other gods are taken to dwell within their houses.

Others understand this more punitively: the Wise One condemns the wicked to the underworld while the good are taken among the gods. Many reject literal life after death entirely, understanding the myth metaphorically — the underworld and the dwellings of the gods represent whether one’s deeds live on in memory.

The Three Great Oracles

  • The Timestrider Oracle of the Swift is the most important. The Swift goes so fast they can run into the future and back again. The Oracle gives delphic predictions.
  • The Dustlistener Oracle of the Sorrowful allows people to contact the dead or learn things about the past. (The Sorrowful is the grief-aspect of the Wise One — see above — and the oracle is reckoned the senior cult-site of that persona.)
  • The Soulshielder Oracle of the Wise dispenses general advice about how to live well. Although seemingly anodyne when written, those who receive this advice in person seem uncannily better able to weather life’s storms.

Racial Connections

  • Giants believe themselves to be the special children of the Strong or the Hearty
  • Halflings believe themselves to be the special children of the Lucky
  • Dragons consider the Old Gods to be for lesser races — they believe they predate the gods

Druid Circles

Several druid circles are associated with the Old Gods:
- A Wayfinder-aligned circle
- A circle of the Swift
- A localist circle focused on place-specific spirits

Prevalence

The Old Gods are the most widely held set of beliefs in Moonrise — roughly 45% of the population are adherents, compared to about 30% for The Congregation, 15% for The Three, 10% for Wayfinders, and 10% who are avowedly atheist. (These figures are rough and overlap somewhat, as many people hold beliefs from more than one tradition.)

Moonrise

Moonrise is a homebrew fantasy world created for a tabletop RPG campaign played over approximately two years using the Dungeon World system. The campaign is now finished, and this wiki serves as a record of the world, its history, and the story that was told in it.

What Makes Moonrise Different

Moonrise is distinguished from standard D&D settings by its moral complexity and religious uncertainty:

  • No proven gods. Religion operates more like the real world. Clerical magic works, but it works even for atheists. The existence of the gods is a matter of faith, not fact — with the striking exception of The Three, who appeared five years ago claiming to be living gods.
  • No Celestials or Devils. Contact with other planes is extremely rare. The cosmological certainties of a standard D&D setting do not apply here.
  • Morally complex factions. Most people in Moonrise think some creatures are inherently good or evil, but that does not mean they are right. Every faction has both virtues and vices; none is straightforwardly heroic or villainous.

The Factions

Ten major political powers shape the world:

Faction Summary
Old Dominion An ancient aristocratic empire ruled by the ghosts of ancestors
The League A loose alliance of human territories bound by a magical Compact
Forest Kingdoms A wood elf feudal realm, deeply connected to the Fey
The Three Living gods who conquered the Lakeland five years ago
The Congregation A religion devoted to the Giver, led by the 160th reincarnation of the Prophet
The Combine A multinational dwarven trading cartel, now multiracial
Giant Clans Warrior clans devoted to honour, feared for their potential to reunite
Dragons Fiercely independent, individually powerful, collectively devastating
The Kin A secret lycanthrope crime syndicate
The Veil Assassins of tyrants

The Peoples

  • Humans — the most numerous, found everywhere
  • Dwarves — Gold and Silver, miners and traders
  • Elves — Wood (Sylvan) and High (Sundered), with very different cultures
  • Halflings — Sand (desert nomads) and Grass (diaspora)
  • Goblins — subterranean rationalists, still bearing the scars of the Ashen Crusade
  • Orcs — many still enslaved, with a rich culture of music and martial arts
  • Dragonborn — rare individuals transformed by service to dragons
  • Genasi — rare individuals infused with elemental energy

Religion

  • The Old Gods — the Sacred Seven, worshipped by ~45% of the population
  • The Congregation — monotheistic religion of the Giver (~30%)
  • The Three — the living gods (~15%)
  • Wayfinders — a philosophy of meditation and inaction (~10%)

Genasi

Genasi are rare individuals created by a ritual infusing a baby in the womb with elemental energy, usually conducted by parents who are devotees of elemental lords. They are generally regarded with suspicion.

In earlier times, only fire-infused individuals were known (sometimes called Tieflings in older texts). Today, all four elemental types are recognised — fire, water, earth, and air — each carrying distinctive physical traits reflecting their element.

Physical traits

Fire genasi run a body temperature noticeably higher than ordinary, with skin that carries the colour of hot iron at the edges of scars and at the palms. Their hair is often genuinely alight in low winds, without burning. Water genasi have damp skin that does not dry, cool to the touch, and in cold weather beads with frost. Earth genasi are heavy-boned, sometimes half a hand taller than would be expected, and often develop mineral crusts at the elbows and knees as they age. Air genasi are the lightest-built; their breath carries an audible note at the edge of hearing, and in still rooms a slight stirring of the air around them can be seen.

None of these traits is cosmetic — they carry real consequences. A fire genasi cannot be concealed in a crowded inn. A water genasi must wring out their clothes on a summer day. These practical facts shape how genasi live.

The ritual

The ritual requires proximity to a zone of elemental influence (see Cosmology): volcanic vents for fire, earthmote fields for air, breathable sea zones for water, deep quarries or sinkholes for earth. It is costly, dangerous to the mother, and fails more often than it succeeds. A failed ritual usually kills both mother and child; the disfigurements of genasi who survive a partial ritual are the reason for much of the general suspicion the ancestry provokes.

Elemental lords are not gods in the Old Gods sense — they are powerful individual elementals who accept worship in exchange for intermittent attention. A cult of the elemental lords will typically include a small number of devotees, a shrine within range of an elemental zone, and a ritualist capable of the infusion. The cults are rarely large enough to attract official persecution but are consistently disliked by the Congregation, which regards elemental veneration as theologically confused.

Community

Because the ritual sites are scattered and the cults small, most genasi grow up alone or with one or two others of their element. Enclaves of any size exist only near the larger elemental zones — the fire-cult settlement of Emberwell on the southern slopes of the Godspires, the water-cult commune on the island of Brindle in the Gentle Sea. A genasi community draws a steady trickle of pilgrims from further afield, people seeking a ritual for an unborn child, and these pilgrims often winter there.

Genasi themselves rarely produce genasi children — the ritual does not pass down. A second-generation genasi exists only where a cultist couple has performed the rite a second time.

Names

Genasi names are almost always first-names only; family names, where they exist, come from the non-genasi parent. First-names are chosen to be literal descriptions of the elemental nature:

  • Fire: Ember, Pyre, Cinder, Coal, Spark, Kettle, Flint (for the spark rather than the stone)
  • Water: Brine, Tidewell, Rill, Foam, Runnel, Deepbuck, Shallow
  • Earth: Quarry, Slate, Vein, Hollow, Boulder, Chalk, Seam
  • Air: Gust, Cirrus, Mistral, Squall, Breath, Thin, Updraft

Work and stereotype

Genasi are thought to be suited to trades aligned with their element, and in practice many gravitate toward these trades because other work is closed to them. Fire genasi are found at forges, kilns, cremation grounds, and — in League cities — in the lamp-trimmers’ guilds. Water genasi are unusually common as ferrymen, dockhands, and apothecaries; the water-genasi trade in preserving fluids is small but lucrative. Earth genasi end up in quarries, under masonry contracts, or as slow-tilling farmers in marginal land. Air genasi are prized as couriers and mountain guides, and at higher altitudes are sometimes the only workers who can function.

These distributions hide the fact that a fire genasi can be a clerk and an air genasi can be a farmer; the stereotypes are strong enough that most genasi find it easier to go along with them than to fight.

Language

Genasi who maintain contact with the elemental planes speak Primordial or one of its dialects (Infernal for fire, Terran for earth, Aquan for water, Auran for air). Most also speak whatever tongue their non-genasi parent speaks, and in that language their names are often half-translated: a genasi called Ember in the League is called Kool in Dutch circles and Glut in Gold Dwarven ones.

Idioms

  • A fire in the womb: a restless or ungovernable child
  • Earth-named: stubborn; by extension, a person who will not be moved from a position
  • To be short of breath (of a person, not a body): in Auran-influenced dialects, to be near the end of patience

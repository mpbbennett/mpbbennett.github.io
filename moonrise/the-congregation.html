The Congregation

Values: Purity, Care
Symbol: White
Characteristic class: Cleric

The Congregation is a religion devoted to the one true god, the Giver. Like The Three, the Congregation is exclusive — in most territories it offers one religion competing among others, while in some it is recognised as a state church. The faith rarely endorses violence against non-believers, though its stance against heresy within is much more robust.

Theology

The Giver exists in a state of eternal conflict with the Deceiver. The two powers are evenly balanced; both are omniscient, but neither is omnipotent. People have free will to choose between them, and the ultimate fate of the universe will be determined by their choice. The Deceiver’s most potent weapon is to convince people that their individual choices are too small to matter, and that they should accordingly do as they like.

In more philosophical texts, the Giver/Deceiver duality is sometimes rephrased as Creation/Entropy, and the two principles are understood as aspects of the soul of the universe rather than person-like entities.

This dualistic worldview developed out of Wayfinder philosophy. However, Wayfinders see both light and dark as valid and necessary parts of the universe, with the goal being balance. Congregants, by contrast, identify goodness with the light and evil with the dark.

The Old Gods are regarded as ignorant attempts to venerate particular aspects of the Giver (or indeed the Deceiver).

Reincarnation and Resurrection

Congregants believe in a cycle of reincarnation, with good conduct leading to rebirth in better circumstances. Believers eventually escape the cycle to dwell with the Giver in heaven. This idea has often been interpreted in a racist way, with some forms of intelligent life regarded as “higher” in the reincarnation hierarchy (elves over humans over greenskins).

Reincarnation happens naturally and people do not remember their past lives. On enlightenment, one leaves the cycle to abide with the Giver. But with magical aid, believers can force their souls back — either into their previous body or into another humanoid baby being born at the time.

Lamas

High Congregation clerics sometimes choose to stay within the world and be reincarnated rather than departing. They choose new bodies because this is “cleaner” than trying to stay in the same ageing body again and again. Clerics who do this as a matter of course are called Lamas. There are seven lesser Lamas and the Great Lama.

Resurrection Magic

Resurrection magic is the exclusive purview of the Congregation. Other religions find it unnatural. The Congregation themselves see it as an intervention in the cycle of rebirth — not done lightly. Resurrection is performed to complete quests the Congregation views as important, and those resurrected are expected to follow through. The faith has made clear that Congregant rulers cannot expect resurrection as a matter of course.

The Prophet

The faith is led by its Prophet, believed to be the same soul reincarnated again and again, delaying their journey to heaven to save the peoples of this world. The current Prophet is a twelve-year-old girl named Antinea, believed to be the 160th reincarnation. She dwells atop the Great Spire in Brightspire, the Congregation’s holy city. When a Prophet dies, the faith scours the globe for a child born at the same moment.

During the Prophet’s minority, the faith is governed by a Curial Council appointed from the Synod of Archprelates. Archprelates are the senior clergy, each responsible for large regions. There are currently 84.

Brightspire

The Congregation’s holy city and the seat of the Prophet. Its landmarks include:

  • The Great Spire — A tower veined with gold, visible for miles. A winding stairway leads to the summit where the Prophet dwells. Pilgrims climb the steps as an act of devotion.
  • The Burning Square — The central plaza, dominated by a perpetual brazier lit centuries ago and never allowed to go out.
  • The River of Reflections — Iridescent stone steps lead down to a sacred river. Priests wade into it to bless pilgrims with “firestones” — obsidian rocks heated red-hot.
  • The Path of Shadows — A deep canyon cut into the desert outside the city, used for spiritual trials (see below).

The city is suffused with prayer flags, chanting, and processions of monks in red and yellow robes carrying flaming banners. Rules for visitors are strict: fasting during daylight, no visible weapons, no swearing, no shoes.

The Path of Shadows

A pilgrimage trial site in a deep canyon near Brightspire. Those who enter face a series of supernatural ordeals:

  • A guardian specter at the Gate of Warnings tests the pilgrim’s resolve, identifying vulnerabilities
  • Shadow wraiths inhabit cracks in the canyon walls, feeding on fear and doubt
  • The Shrine of Ashes offers a reignitable ember that restores strength to exhausted pilgrims
  • The Chasm of Reflection fills with mist that manifests doppelgangers — shadow versions of the pilgrim embodying their hidden aspects (pride, doubt, guilt, fear)
  • The Flame of Renewal brazier at the end requires a personal sacrifice to ignite

Successfully completing the Path is considered a profound spiritual achievement.

The Darkened Well

Hidden within the Path of Shadows is a site for healing blindness. Pilgrims drink pitch-black water from an obsidian chalice. The water grants sight, but leaves the eyes permanently tinged gray and creates a lingering connection to shadow and darkness.

Monastic Orders

In addition to the regular clergy, there are many monastic orders. The most famous are the four orders of friars, who are often responsible for running hospitals and schools. All ordained Congregation clergy (called Friars) shave their heads and wear long white or cream-coloured robes.

The Unseers

A temple order whose members ritually sacrifice their sight. Members bear burn marks where their eyes should be, yet can see — a paradox they attribute to the Giver’s light replacing mortal vision. Their temple, the Temple of the Unseen Light, is ringed by embers surrounding a central blue-white brazier. In the initiation ritual, a supplicant places an offering in the brazier and allows the flame to travel up their arm, “consuming darkness” — cruelty, greed, and selfishness. If sincere, the supplicant’s shadow detaches and speaks, trying to persuade them to keep their vices. Those who endure gain sight without eyes.

Vernacular Congregation worship revolves around cults of saints, often a way of absorbing and repurposing older local gods and heroes.

Religious Practice

Several demonstrations of faith are expected of Congregants:

  • Prayer thrice a day (sunrise, noon, sunset) — gongs ring out a call to prayer
  • Pilgrimage to the Holy City in which the Prophet resides
  • Giving a tithe of income to the faith, distributed as alms to the poor
  • A month of fasting and a weekly fast day
  • Vegetarian diet

Congregant Rituals

Congregant rituals focus on holy fire, with each temple centred on a sacred flame which must be kept burning at all times. More elaborate rituals to consecrate greater temples require increasingly exotic sources of fire — lightning bolts, dragon breath, volcanic eruptions, fire from the house of a king.

History

The Congregation is roughly 700 years old. It emerged in the aftermath of the cataclysm that destroyed the High Elven Empire, when the first Prophet appeared. Many high elves flocked to the Congregation to atone for the violence and hubris of their shattered empire. The Congregation accordingly inherits much of the prestige and institutional structure of the old empire.

The faith saw significant growth when an Arcane Sovereign attempted to impose it as a state religion.

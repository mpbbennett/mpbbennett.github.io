Giant Clans

Values: Loyalty, Authority
Symbol: Red footprint
Characteristic class (Goliaths): Barbarian

Giants are devoted to clan and honour above all else. Other peoples live in fear that the giants will unite again, but only a powerful overlord can halt their endless infighting.

Society

Giant society is organised by caste and clan. There is a hierarchy of six castes: Storm, Cloud, Fire, Frost, Stone, and Hill. Within each caste there are around ten clans. Loyalty is first and foremost to one’s clan, secondarily to the caste, and only lastly to giantkind as a whole.

Giants are expected to be always obedient to their clanleaders. The only exception is if they believe their leaders are bringing disaster upon the clan itself — a theme frequently played for tragic effect in giant sagas. While obedience is expected to a sitting leader, leadership is open upon death to the strongest contender.

Naming

Giants are traditionally given the name of the first thing they set eyes upon after birth. Names are normally three words at most: Sky-of-Rain, Bloody-Spear, Flickering-Flame. Some clans engineer what children see so they can be named accordingly, while others make it a principle to avoid such manipulations.

Population

Giants are naturally long-lived but very slow to reproduce. Each clan has around 150 individuals (children and elderly included), for a total population of only about 9,000.

Why Giants Don’t Rule the World

Despite their immense martial power, giants have rarely established lasting dominance over other peoples, for three reasons:

  1. Small population. Very slow reproduction limits their numbers.
  2. No collaboration. Their tribalistic culture makes giant empires reluctant to engage with the elites of conquered peoples. Without institutions or local collaborators, giant empires have been brittle and short-lived.
  3. Clan loyalty. The intensity of clan loyalties makes it difficult to unite the clans into a conquering force in the first place.

Nonetheless, the sheer martial power of giantkind has given rise to several empires, most notably those founded by Ossin and Morgru.

The Morgrian Reform

The current system was instituted by Morgru in the Morgrian Reform, which broke up several clans and created new ones for a plan of six castes each with ten clans. Since the fall of the Morgrian empire, several clans have split and several have been destroyed, so the arrangement no longer perfectly reflects the original plan.

Goliaths

Goliaths are a half-human, half-giant race, with characteristics much closer to humans in size. Nobody is quite sure how interbreeding first occurred — scholars hypothesise magic must have been involved, but asking a goliath for the details is a sure way to have one’s skull caved in. Goliaths can reproduce with other goliaths and with humans.

A few giant clans include goliath members, while others have affiliated goliath tribes. Goliaths often serve as go-betweens between giants and other races.

Cyclopes

Cyclopes are supposedly related to giants, though no-one knows the details. Compared to giants, they are something closer to wild animals, with lower intelligence and a much more solitary way of life. Despite this, they are generally peaceful unless provoked.

Unclanned Giants

Not all giants adhere to clan society. Those outside are known as “unclanned” — sometimes by choice, sometimes due to disgrace. Some live in the wilds; others live among other peoples, often finding work as mercenaries or in construction and transportation.

Foreign Relations

Giants are generally feared, and other peoples often ally to keep them confined to their current lands. Raids on surrounding territories are very common, and tributary relationships sometimes arise. A reunited giant empire is the nightmare of many statesmen. Spymasters look for opportunities to sow disunity among the giants, but this is difficult given their famous distrust of outsiders.

Giants are also often employed as mercenaries. Some whole clans operate as mercenary companies, while others allow squads to hire themselves out.

Religion

Giants believe themselves to be the special children of one particular god — either the Strong or the Hearty — among the Old Gods.

Goals

Long-term (majority): Restoration of giant overlordship.
Long-term (minority): Return to the original giant homeland.

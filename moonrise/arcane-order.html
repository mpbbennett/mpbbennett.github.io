Names and Naming

Arcane Order

The Arcane Order was a magocratic empire that ruled much of Moonrise for approximately four centuries, from roughly 550 years ago until its fall to the giant invasion 160 years before the campaign’s start. Also recorded in older texts as the Imperium Arcanum or the Arcane Sovereignty, the Order arose from the wreckage of the Ashen Crusade era and stood as the world’s dominant political power until the westward giant migration made its position untenable.

At campaign start (103AC), the Order no longer exists as a governing body. Its living remnant — a reduced community of mages, examination institutions, and archival traditions — persists in scattered form across the Central Valley region, heavily subordinated to the successor states of The League.

Origins

The Order did not emerge from a single coup or conquest. It coalesced over time from an alliance of three distinct forces: a magocratic city-state, an established wizarding order, and a number of independent archmages who shared a common interest in stable government after the disorder that followed the Ashen Crusade.

The coalition’s first approach was indirect. Rather than rule themselves, the allied mages attempted to secure reliable governance by sponsoring suitable rulers. The first king they elevated proved ineffective. The second became tyrannical. The mages concluded that placing power in others’ hands was not working and created the Sovereignty directly, with the Archmage-Sovereign as its head.

Government and Hierarchy

The Order was a ranked meritocracy. The ruling elite comprised magic users organised in a formal hierarchy:

  • Archmage-Sovereign — supreme executive, formally chosen by their predecessor but in practice negotiated with the archmagi
  • Archmagi — three subranks; the effective ruling council
  • Magi — twelve subranks; the senior civil service and judicial class
  • Adepts — four subranks; junior officials and examination candidates

Below all of these were the “clods” — those without magical ability, comprising roughly ninety percent of the population. Ranks entitled their holders to wear distinctive patterned robes; the system was visible and publicly legible.

Advancement was through state-administered examinations covering wizardry, history, philosophy, and statecraft. The examination system was largely impartial, though corruption scandals recurred across the Order’s history. Formal title above Adept required both magical and scholarly competence.

In practice, the Sovereignty was more collegial than its formal structure implied. Sovereigns who stood against united archmage opposition did not remain Sovereigns long; many came to what were officially described as magical accidents. The archmagi proved willing to apply the same ruthlessness to their own leaders that they had once applied to the kings they’d sponsored.

The Order’s meritocracy created persistent tension with the feudal nobility of conquered territories. Nobles resented being required to defer to the commands of individuals they regarded as social inferiors, regardless of those individuals’ examination results. Common people, by contrast, generally benefited from competent administration and were broadly supportive.

Magic and the Schools

The Order classified magical practice through its Schools — a formal curriculum structure that also served as the basis for the examination ladder. Elemental magic was a recognised specialism within this system; the most accomplished elementalists worked with the binding of efreeti and the permanent construction of earth-bound golems. Arianell, the Grandmaster condemned to elemental cessation approximately 250 years ago, was described in contemporary sources as the most accomplished elementalist of his generation, with a private menagerie of bound elementals and the capacity to construct large-scale permanent golems.

The etherwalk rite, documented in the third book of the Order’s Schools, permitted practitioners to move through the Border Ethereal. The Order maintained a standing committee on planar theory, including a minority tradition that rejected the nested-ring cosmological model in favour of a branching diagram.

Mages operating outside the Order’s territories today still navigate guild systems that are a direct legacy of the Sovereignty’s regulatory apparatus. League rulers, mindful of the Order’s example, structure their Mage Guilds specifically to prevent the horizontal association among practitioners that might reconstitute an independent magical power bloc.

The Cloud-Capital

The Order’s capital was a flying city constructed on linked earthmotes — segments of stone drawn from the Plane of Air — and connected by bridges. The city was built incrementally across many decades, with new earthmotes added and joined to the existing structure. At peak operation it maintained four public teleportation circles, each attended by a mage responsible for deploying an antimagical field to prevent collisions during embarkation and disembarkation. Pre-booking was required at high-traffic times.

The capital’s mobility was a deliberate instrument of governance: the Sovereign could relocate the seat of power as political conditions required, projecting authority to any part of the empire without requiring a fixed administrative capital elsewhere.

After the Order’s fall, Sky-of-Rain yoked her cloud giant castle to the Cloud-Capital. During the Clan Wars, rebels downed the entire structure, causing massive loss of life. The city still lies in ruins in an inaccessible location. Its archive vaults — holding the working papers of every Archmage — survived partially intact and remain a target for treasure-hunters and scholars. See The Cloud-Capital for further detail.

Notable Sovereigns

Caradoc the Pious attempted to impose the Congregation as a state religion. The attempt failed, but the Congregation grew significantly during his reign — a result he appears to have regarded as partial success.

Gethin the Callous purchased the orc race as slaves from the Giants to work on the Order’s great engineering projects. His reign was characterised by brutality and paranoia; he survived multiple assassination attempts before being killed by a member of The Veil, reportedly with the knowledge of some archmagi.

Cadfael the Lame was the most powerful wizard of the age, elevated reluctantly to the Sovereignty after the Callous’s assassination. Born without the use of their legs, they moved using levitation magic. When every humanoid in Moonrise experienced a simultaneous nightmare — the Night of Anguish — the Lame abdicated, set sail alone for the Eastern Isles, and was never seen again. They are now remembered as a model of the reluctant ruler — powerful beyond any rival, yet willing to surrender power entirely the moment they judged it necessary.

Notable Archmagi

Arianell, a Grandmaster of the Order, was condemned to elemental cessation approximately 250 years ago after publishing memoranda alleging a Faerie–Giant conspiracy against the Order and unilaterally hiring Giant mercenaries to garrison Order sites. The council that condemned him regarded the mercenary action — placing armed non-Order forces inside Order infrastructure — as the decisive breach. The subsequent open Giant invasion under Sky-of-Rain, which began roughly thirty years after Arianell’s death, has generated a persistent rehabilitation literature arguing he was suppressed for telling an inconvenient truth.

Decline and Fall

The giant migration westward began incrementally, almost a century before the Order’s collapse. The Order’s initial response was integration: new arrivals were absorbed into the military and redistributed across the empire. By the time Sky-of-Rain arrived with the final wave, nearly three-quarters of the Order’s non-magical forces were giants.

Sky-of-Rain declared her intent to conquer. Most of the giant soldiers in Order service mutinied. The Order’s fall then proceeded quickly, aided by feudal nobles who backed the invasion hoping for higher standing under a warrior-aristocratic regime, the Bleeding King’s seizure of the Lakeland, and a major slave revolt among the orcs of the Isthmus. The archmagi, characteristically pragmatic, surrendered relatively quickly once the cause appeared lost.

The Clan Wars that followed Sky-of-Rain’s death further shattered the Order’s surviving institutions and killed much of what remained of its administrative class.

Legacy

The Order’s examination-and-rank system left permanent marks on the successor states. The League’s Mage Guilds are structured explicitly as a reaction to the Order — tools for the control of magic-users rather than platforms for their self-organisation. The League’s Compact law, magically sealed and enforced through its Basilica court system, echoes the Order’s approach to legally binding obligations.

The Combine has been quietly attempting to acquire Order-era artefacts and records, particularly anything related to automata technology — a search complicated by the fact that most surviving Order archives are in the inaccessible Cloud-Capital ruins. Several archmagi’s estates and research sites remain unexcavated in the former Order heartland.

The Order’s two great unsolved failures — the Night of Anguish, which ended Cadfael’s reign, and the Arianell affair — continue to generate scholarly dispute. Whether the Faerie–Giant coordination Arianell described was real, and what caused the Night of Anguish, remain open questions.

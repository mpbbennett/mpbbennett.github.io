High Elven Empire

The High Elven Empire was the great power of the deep past — a dynastic empire of high elves that, by the fragmentary accounts that survive, ruled most of the known world for many centuries before its sudden fall, well over a thousand years before campaign start.

Almost nothing about it is known with confidence. No living being remembers it: even the longest-lived elves, whose memories reach some three centuries, fall many lifetimes short of the Empire’s age. What survives is documentary and partial — scattered references in Cloud-Capital-era archives and Congregation libraries, copied and recopied, agreeing on little beyond the broadest outline.

That outline: the Empire was vast, its court magic more capable than anything achieved since, and it ended in a single catastrophe. The nature of the catastrophe is disputed and probably unrecoverable. Surviving accounts call it a natural cataclysm, but for a power of that magical capacity the line between natural and magical disaster is not one the sources can be trusted to draw. The subterranean Drow are named in connection with the fall in nearly every account that mentions both — but how, and whether as cause or consequence, is nowhere explained.

Legacy

The fall scattered the high elves across the continent, where they remain a small diaspora. Many read the Empire’s destruction as a judgement on its hubris and, in later centuries, flocked to the Congregation; the faith inherited much of the Empire’s prestige and institutional culture, and with it a prominent high elven clergy. Two older institutions are also held to date from the imperial period: the Wayfinder philosophical tradition, and the High Kingship of the wood Elves that would in time become the Forest Kingdoms. A single high elven city is said to survive somewhere remote, small and turned in on itself — though even this is closer to rumour than report.

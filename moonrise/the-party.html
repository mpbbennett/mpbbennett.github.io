The Party

The adventuring party whose exploits shaped the recent history of Moonrise. The campaign ran for approximately 52 sessions using the Dungeon World system, beginning in Arrowmoor on 26 Swiftmoon 103AC.

Members

Lucien (Bard)

A charismatic bard and the party’s face. Lucien had connections across the region — he counted the Count of Merrykeep as a contact, knew a priest in the Lakeland, and was shadowing a Dr Lavena. He had a romance with Gwendolen and later an interest in Dafne at the New Academy.

Lucien’s talent was real — he could put priestesses into trances with his music, calm krakens with his singing, and charm his way out of almost any situation. He composed a famous ballad about the party’s defeat of a giant snake. He acquired a magical invisible accordion that proved key to solving several puzzles.

During the climactic encounter with the reality-altering Cube, Lucien used his charm to distract the Lich Arianell with flattery while the others worked to undo his changes. He also edited reality to make himself the most famous bard in the world — a characteristically selfish and successful gambit.

Epilogue: In Vermillion city, an aged Lucien plays to a vast crowd whose cheering drowns out his music. Surrounded by fans and admirers, he sits alone afterwards and toasts a painting of Esmerelda — but the painting does not move. Esmerelda, a sentient painting he had tried to give a body, was transformed by the procedure into a strange creature who withdrew to a mountain cave. Fame without genuine connection.

Caesar / Elsbeth (Rogue / Lycanthrope)

A rogue and assassin who went by multiple identities. Originally “Caesar,” she had worked with The Kin before being betrayed, murdered a gang leader’s son to escape, and gone into hiding. Her past caught up with her throughout the campaign.

Elsbeth was turned into a lycanthrope (werebear) mid-campaign when bitten by an uncontrolled werewolf. This forced a deeper reckoning with her nature. At Brightspire, she made a deliberate choice to embrace the light, putting her assassin blades into the fire and swearing she was done with her old ways. Her sight was restored.

She killed The Carver (the Kin Boss in Arrowmoor) and took over his identity, then brokered a deal with the Kin to transport freed slaves. In the Cube encounter, Elsbeth made the crucial edit that reversed Arianell’s changes, effectively defeating him.

Epilogue: Kadir (a Kin operative) tracked her down for revenge. In the fight, Elsbeth lost her sight after killing an unarmed henchman. She joined Father Marcellus to help the needy. Her sight returned, but a vision of Kadir attacking sent her back into darkness — this time for good.

Magnus (Warrior)

A formidable warrior cursed with a sentient sword that demanded blood. Magnus was prone to violent impulses — early in the campaign, he killed a captive priestess mid-interrogation, and later mauls bystanders during uncontrolled transformations.

Magnus became a lycanthrope (werebear) and later converted Caesar into one as well. He adopted a bear companion, Sir Bearington, who accompanied him through the later campaign. At the Mirror of Heaven, Magnus endured a gruelling trial in the desert sun to break the sword’s curse — succeeding when the sword acknowledged his honour and thanked him for restoring his family name.

In the Cube encounter, Magnus edited reality to fight Arianell in a duel on a misty mountaintop, dealing him substantial damage before being fixed into the mountainside by Arianell’s dying spell.

Epilogue: Magnus returned home and took charge from his younger brother. He continued practising swordwork, adopting a more military bearing. Sir Bearington retired to a cave with a family of bears.

Storr (Silver Dwarf)

A Silver Dwarf and member of the Nidevilir Wolves, who had worked as a debt collector for The Combine and done forge work at Ulrik and Mette’s. Storr’s driving quest was finding his lost family members.

He travelled to the Scalewind Isles and struck a deal with the dragon Blazejaw, who revealed that Storr’s family had been petrified by a Medusa. Throughout the campaign, Storr was the reliable presence — smashing doors, breaking chains, keeping the party grounded.

In the Cube encounter, Storr tried to rescue his family by editing reality so they had never left — but he failed, unable to remember their faces, creating a dangerous causal contradiction.

Epilogue: The party defeated the Gorgon to free Storr’s family, but he still struggled to remember them. Storr returned to the mines, dreaming about the true name of Blazejaw — but never quite remembering when he woke. Blazejaw, perched on a scorched mountain, chuckled remembering Storr.

Aldara (Wizard / Archmage)

A wizard driven by the pursuit of knowledge, specifically seeking the Tomb of Archmage Arianell. Aldara was systematic and methodical, using ritual magic, spirit contact, and careful research rather than brute force.

At the Cloud-Capital, Aldara was formally elevated to Archmage — but the title came with the legal inheritance of the Arcane Order’s rights, debts, obligations, and a Curator follower who reminded him constantly of his responsibilities.

In the Cube encounter, Aldara systematically located the memory where they had awakened the Lich and began deleting it. The Curator integrated irreversibly with the Cube to help. Arianell fought back by editing Aldara’s own memory, but Aldara counterattacked with logical contradiction.

Epilogue: As Grand Mage, Aldara found himself buried in administrative paperwork. Being a grand mage was not working out as he had hoped. Worse, there was whispering from the farsight orb — it sounded like Arianell laughing.

The Campaign’s Climax

The campaign built to a confrontation with Arianell, a Lich who was originally an Archmage of the old Arcane Order. He sought to use the Worldshaper Cube — an ancient goblin artifact hidden in a vault beneath the mountains — to edit reality itself.

The party discovered the Cube could alter memories and events, but changes that contradicted reality created dangerous “causal instabilities.” In a tense sequence, each party member used the Cube in turn. Lucien charmed the Lich as a distraction. Aldara deleted the memories that awakened Arianell. Elsbeth reversed Arianell’s recent changes. Magnus fought him in a duel on a mountaintop. Storr tried and failed to save his family.

Arianell’s last words were: “Very clever — but you will never know what else I changed…”

Aftermath

The party’s reality-editing created lasting consequences:
- Intense deja vu and unreliable memories across Moonrise
- Enhanced and more unpredictable magic
- Weakened boundaries between planes, leading to interplanar travellers
- Arianell’s lingering presence — cloud patterns resembling him, wind sounding like his voice

Playstyle

The campaign was described as “Indiana Jones action, Game of Thrones politics” — fairly lethal, with gender not a factor but race very much so. The system was Dungeon World (Powered by the Apocalypse), not D&D 5e.

Key Locations

The campaign began in Arrowmoor (Birchfield village) and ranged across the duchy — Inchcliff, Graveport, Thornbush Town, Gillfern, Orlane, and St. Crispin’s Monastery — before expanding to wider Moonrise including Mistdam, the Scalewind Isles, the Isthmian Games at Vermillion and Azure, Brightspire, and the ruins of The Cloud-Capital.

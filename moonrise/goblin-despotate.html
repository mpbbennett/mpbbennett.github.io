Names and Naming

Goblin Despotate

The Goblin Despotate was a goblin imperial state that existed from roughly 750 to 545 years before campaign start, making it the largest empire in recorded Moonrise history at its height. It emerged from a single ruling house’s discovery that automata — previously an artisanal curiosity — could be manufactured and deployed at military scale, and it fell when the demands it placed on its subject peoples provoked the rebellion known as the Ashen Crusade.

The Despotate is now extinct as a state. Modern goblin society is a diaspora of enclaves and gobtowns operating within other peoples’ polities. Long-standing rumours hold that the Despotate’s ruling structures were never fully destroyed and that certain goblin interests still exercise hidden influence over surface kingdoms.

The Golden Age

Before the Despotate, goblin civilisation consisted of a network of underground city-states, each controlling its surrounding territory but without serious ambitions over the wider world. These city-states were renowned for technological innovation — particularly the construction of automata: mechanical constructs built for labour, record-keeping, and, eventually, war.

The city-states were prosperous and philosophically sophisticated. Goblin names follow Greek conventions, and philosophy was the prestige intellectual discipline. The Beholder Tiles boardgame, now played across much of Moonrise and claimed by both goblins and Dwarves as their own invention, was a product of this period.

Automata in the golden age were artisanal — individually constructed, expensive, and maintained by specialist craftspeople within a single city. The conceptual leap that created the Despotate was not the automaton itself but the scaling of its production: treating automata construction as an industrial process rather than a craft.

Rise of the Despotate

One ruling house — its name not preserved in surviving sources outside goblin archives — developed the techniques and institutions needed for large-scale automaton manufacture, maintenance, and battlefield deployment. With this advantage, it began a programme of military expansion: first against non-goblin neighbouring territories, then against the other goblin city-states, and finally against the wider continent.

The Despotate grew to become the largest empire in recorded history. Its administration was sophisticated; the city-states it absorbed retained local governance structures, supervised by Despotate officials who collected taxation and military levies. The technical knowledge required to build and maintain the automaton armies remained concentrated among specialist goblin craftspeople, giving the ruling house a permanent advantage over potential rivals.

The Wood Elf War

The scaling of the automaton army created a structural problem: automata required specific construction materials in short supply. Maintaining the existing army required continuous acquisition of these materials; expanding it required even more. The Despotate was therefore compelled to expand its territorial reach beyond what its political consolidation had otherwise required.

This imperative drove the Despotate into the wood elf forests — a campaign that became the defining catastrophe of the late Despotate period. The forests were defended by a decentralised guerrilla insurgency for which the Despotate’s hulking automata were poorly suited. Automata optimised for open-field set-piece battles performed badly in forest terrain: too large to move quietly, too rigid to adapt to ambush tactics.

The Despotate’s response was escalation. Rather than adapt its military doctrine, it began systematically burning forest tracts to remove the terrain advantage from the elven defenders. The burning created a cycle of increasing brutality on both sides. Elven resistance stiffened as the destruction of the forests intensified. The campaign drained Despotate resources and morale without achieving decisive victory.

Subject Peoples and the Mutiny

The wood elf war required the Despotate to intensify its demands on its non-goblin subject territories. Military drafts pulled soldiers from human, dwarven, high elven, and halfling populations. Taxation increased to fund the campaign.

Resentment accumulated. The breaking point came when a large conscript army — assembled from multiple subject peoples and sent to fight in the elf forests — mutinied in the field. The soldiers killed their goblin commanders and joined the elven forces they had been ordered to fight. This was not the first instance of local resistance, but it was the first to break an entire field army and reverse its allegiance.

The mutiny grew into a full-scale rebellion across the subject territories. The Despotate found itself defending its original territories rather than projecting power outward, and fell back on its underground fortresses.

The Ashen Crusade

A half-elf leader of the rebel forces underwent a mystical experience at a volcanic temple dedicated to an aspect of the Strong One. He reported a divine command to eradicate goblinkind. He executed goblin captives, bathed in their blood, and covered himself in ash — a ritual self-marking he declared would remain until his work was done.

The resulting Ashen Crusade broke the Despotate. Its automaton armies, already overstretched and running short of construction materials, could not hold against a multi-front insurgency backed by the wood elves and the subject peoples. The underground city-states fell one by one.

The Crusade then turned genocidal. The leader’s forces pursued goblin survivors into the deep tunnels, attacked goblin communities in territory not previously part of the Despotate, and targeted populations with no connection to the automaton war. The distinction between military campaign and extermination campaign dissolved.

The leader was eventually assassinated by his own close confidants, sickened by his escalating fanaticism. Most historians agree the killing was assisted by newly emerging rulers of the liberated territories who wanted to consolidate their own power rather than continue an open-ended ideological campaign. He was killed before his stated goal was achieved.

The Warforged

Late in the Ashen Crusade, the Despotate’s surviving engineers developed what was later described in rumour as a “super-weapon.” In fact, it was not a weapon: it was a set of intelligent automata, far more sophisticated than anything previously constructed, designed not to fight but to preserve goblin culture in the event of total eradication.

These Warforged were sealed in a vault beneath the mountains, set to open after two thousand years. Four Warforged known as the Wardens were left active to stand guard. The vault remains sealed. The Combine has been searching for it; the current Director’s pursuit of the Warforged has become consuming to the point of obsession.

Aftermath

The Despotate’s collapse left goblin civilisation shattered. The surviving goblins were too scattered and too depleted to rebuild independent city-states. They gradually dispersed into human settlements, becoming an urban minority living in partitioned quarters — gobtowns — in other peoples’ cities. Some goblin fortresses in the north survived as semi-independent remnants, but these were never capable of reconstituting anything resembling the Despotate’s power.

Dwarves were the primary beneficiaries of the territorial and economic vacuum left by the Despotate’s fall. Dwarven holds expanded into former goblin underground territories. Dwarves absorbed goblin mining and machinery technologies, and eventually the broader commercial and industrial economic niche goblins had occupied. The calculating, rationalist tendency of contemporary dwarven culture owes much to this inheritance — a debt most dwarves do not acknowledge publicly.

The Ashen Crusade casts a long shadow. Goblins are treated with suspicion and hostility in most parts of Moonrise as of 103AC, despite the Despotate’s fall occurring over 500 years before campaign start. The rumour that the Despotate was never fully eradicated — that goblins still control certain kingdoms through hidden influence — is persistent and impossible to fully verify.

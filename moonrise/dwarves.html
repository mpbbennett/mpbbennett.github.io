Names and Naming

Dwarves

Dwarves are divided into two main groups: the Gold Dwarves of the Godspire Mountains and the Silver Dwarves of the Scalewind Isles.

Gold Dwarves

The more numerous branch. Wealthy, deep-delving, and darker complexioned. The Gold Dwarves inhabit the great holds of the Godspire Mountains and are the heartland of The Combine.

The Holds

Each Gold Dwarf hold has its own character:

  • One hold where the dwarves actually build on the surface, with noted terraced agriculture (like Machu Picchu)
  • One where buildings are carved into the stone but streets are on the surface (like Markarth)
  • One built in natural caverns
  • An unusually deep hold, exclusively sustained by mushroom agriculture
  • A hold with a wide cavern neighbourhood that slopes downwards in a massive spiral staircase
  • A standard dwarven hold

The hold which serves as the Combine’s headquarters has gone so far in embracing the outer world that a human town has been built at the top of the hold. Other dwarves are contemptuous of this.

Government

Dwarven holds are ruled by a hereditary lord who shares power with major guilds, associations, and temples. A fundamental part of dwarven culture is that any group of dwarves who live together year-round have the right to govern their own affairs. Dwarven hold leaders are variously translated as “king” or “lord,” but in the dwarven language this is the same word.

Dwarves have never been great empire builders, because of this strong norm of communal self-government.

Silver Dwarves

Less numerous, hardy, heavy-drinking, and lighter complexioned. The Silver Dwarves inhabit the Scalewind Isles and have a distinctive relationship with Dragons.

Silver Dwarves have a habit of swapping delves with dragons — a dragon will conquer a delve, and the dwarves will conquer a dragon’s lair in turn. This gives them a healthy competitive respect for dragons, but contempt for what they see as the slavish Dragonborn.

The Legendary Holds

Four legendary dwarven holds are regarded today as myths from prehistory: Diamondhold, Rubyhold, Sapphirehold, and Emeraldhold. In fact, these holds were real — overrun by aberrations in the deep past and lost far beneath the earth.

  • Sapphirehold — in the deep south, below the glaciers of Icewyrm. Still inhabited and thriving, but cut off from the world by hostile climate and Dragons, becoming a place of legend to the Scalewind dwarves
  • Rubyhold — built inside an active volcano. Now overrun by fire giants
  • Emeraldhold — built beneath the desert. Still inhabited, but gone eerily strange. Controlled by mind flayers
  • Diamondhold — built very, very deep beneath the surface. Fabulous treasure. Overrun by aberrations

Some great object of power requires components from all four holds.

Gold Dwarven architecture draws on Inca traditions — monumental stonework built to endure for millennia.

The names of dwarven cities need no translation in other languages, because the words for gemstones in Common and other tongues all derive from the original Dwarven.

Culture

Dwarves tend to be dour and cautious except while drunk, which is often. Gold Dwarves drink mushroom brandy and enjoy tobacco. Their characteristic art form is tragic drama, their dress is simple — tunic and trousers — and their favourite game is chess, known as the Dragon Game, which is also beloved by goblins. The Godspire Mountains export gold, iron, gypsum, and coal.

The Goblin Debt

The great secret of dwarven civilisation is the extent of its cultural debt to the Goblins. After the collapse of goblin civilisation, dwarves took over not only goblin territory and technologies of mining and machinery, but the broader economic niche of mining, industry, and commerce. Even the calculating, rationalist streak in modern dwarven culture owes much to goblin influence.

This influence is exemplified in the board game of Beholder Tiles, a national sport for both races. Impartial scholars attribute the game to goblins, but most dwarves cling to dubious theories that dwarves invented it.

Of course, there are also profoundly un-goblin-like aspects to dwarven culture: their love of hard drinking, long grudges, and sentimental attachments to master-crafted items.

Language

Gold Dwarven and Silver Dwarven are mutually intelligible dialects of the same language family and share a writing system. Gold Dwarven names follow a patronymic convention (e.g. “Storr son of Bjorn”), with names drawn from Germanic traditions. Silver Dwarven naming follows Danish conventions. Dwarven is also used as the basis for Draconic script.

Religion

The dwarves originally revered the spirits of the mountains in which they made their homes. Over time, religions from the wider world have entered the holds and become common — the Old Gods, the Congregation, and the Wayfinders all have dwarven adherents. The dwarven creation myth involves the various gods helping to carve the dwarves from stone and giving them various gifts.

History

The War of the Beard was a vicious conflict between the Greenspire Dwarves and the wood Elves, beginning as a local feud over tree-cutting and escalating into a great war. The costly war ended in stalemate, and since then leaders have kept the two peoples separate, though cultural mistrust remains.

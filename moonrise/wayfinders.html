Wayfinders

The Wayfinders (known as Pathfinders in older texts) constitute a religion in a looser sense than The Congregation or even the Old Gods — they are often described as a way of life or philosophy rather than a faith. They emphasise meditation, peace, and the value of inaction.

Beliefs

The Wayfinder tradition is highly decentralised and varied. Three broad schools of thought exist:

  • Atheist Wayfinders claim the gods are a lie and that true insight comes only from within.
  • Syncretic Wayfinders fully integrate the Wayfinder path with worship of the Old Gods, treating the gods as different manifestations of the one path.
  • Transcendent Wayfinders hold that the gods are real and powerful in this world, but that true liberation means going beyond this world and its gods.

Around 10% of the population of Moonrise identify as Wayfinders.

Origins

The Wayfinder tradition originated roughly 2,000 years ago, during the height of the High Elven Empire.

Institutions

Wayfinders are institutionally expressed mainly through monastic communities (sometimes organised in networks of mother and daughter houses) and shrines to Wayfinder sages. Wayfinder monasteries have rich traditions of martial arts.

Wayfinder monastics are called Monks. They leave their hair uncut, with older monks often wearing their very long hair and beards in plaits, braids, or dreadlocks.

Funerary Customs

The Wayfinder funerary custom is for the dead to be placed in “spires of silence” — high towers or clifftops where the bodies may be dismembered by carrion birds. This practice reflects the Wayfinder belief in returning the body to the natural world without ceremony or attachment.

Druid Circles

A Wayfinder-aligned druid circle exists, integrating druidic practice with Wayfinder philosophy.

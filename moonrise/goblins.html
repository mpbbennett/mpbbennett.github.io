Names and Naming

Goblins

Goblins are still widely distrusted even though the rule of the tyrannical Goblin Despotate was violently ended some 800 years ago. Modern goblin culture is subterranean, rationalist, and atheist.

Culture

Goblins tend to be logical and dispassionate. Most are atheist. Some successful goblins are bankers, a trade which is a religious taboo for other peoples. Many rulers have a “Court Goblin” who manages their finances.

Most goblins live in enclaves within human settlements — dingy city quarters called “gobtowns,” often partitioned from the rest of the town. In some regions, goblin fortresses survive, though rarely as fully independent states.

Goblin culture values philosophy as its highest art form, and cards are their favourite entertainment. Their architecture draws on Petra — monumental facades carved into rock faces, recalling the underground city-states of their golden age. Goblin dress follows Napoleonic conventions. Goblins speak Goblish, a language isolate with no close relatives, and use Greek naming conventions.

The boardgame of Beholder Tiles is a national sport for goblins and Dwarves alike. Impartial scholars attribute the game’s invention to goblins, though most dwarves dispute this.

Hobgoblins

Hobgoblins have mixed human and goblin ancestry. They are best known today for their role alongside goblins in banking and lending.

History

The Golden Age

In their golden age, goblins were concentrated in a collection of city-states, each controlling surrounding territory but without serious imperial ambitions. These city-states (constructed largely underground) were famous for their technological innovation, including the construction of automata.

The Goblin Despotate

The military potential of automata was realised by one ruling house, who developed techniques for large-scale construction and deployment of automata in warfare. This launched a period of imperial expansion — first against non-goblin territories, then other goblin city-states, and finally the wider world. The Despotate grew to become the largest empire in the known world.

However, automata construction required materials in short supply. To maintain its army, the Despotate was forced to expand ever further. This led to a long war to conquer the wood elf forests — but the hulking automata were poorly suited for guerrilla warfare in forest terrain. The goblins responded by burning large tracts of forest, creating a cycle of increasing brutality.

The Ashen Crusade

The Despotate’s demands on its subject territories (military drafts, heavy taxation) sparked resentment. A large conscript army mutinied, killed its goblin commanders, and threw in its lot with the wood elves. This mutiny grew into full-scale rebellion.

A half-elf leader had a mystical experience at a volcanic temple and proclaimed a divine command to cleanse the land of goblinkind. Bathing in goblin blood and covering himself in ash, he led what became known as the Ashen Crusade — first breaking the Despotate, then pursuing genocide against the remnants. He was eventually assassinated by his own confidants, sickened by his fanaticism, possibly with the connivance of emerging rulers who preferred to consolidate power rather than pursue an ideological crusade.

Long-standing rumours persist that the Despotate was never truly eradicated and that goblins now control kingdoms as puppet masters from behind the scenes.

The Warforged

The rumoured goblin super-weapon developed late in the Ashen Crusade was in fact the Warforged — intelligent automata created not as weapons but to carry on goblin culture and values in the face of what they believed would be total eradication. The Warforged were sealed in a vault beneath the mountains, set to open two thousand years hence. Four Warforged known as the Wardens were left to stand guard.

Legacy

The Ashen Crusade cast a long shadow. Goblins are treated with suspicion and hostility in many places to this day. The Combine is secretly seeking to recover ancient goblin automata technology — an effort kept clandestine because of the suspicion the technology attracts, especially given its goblin origins. Ancient automata dungeons and goblin research archives still exist in remote locations, drawing treasure-hunters and Combine agents alike.

The great secret of dwarven culture is the extent of its debt to goblins — dwarves took over not only goblin territory and technologies but the broader economic niche goblins had dominated.

Dragons

Values: Purity, Justice
Symbol: Dragon
Characteristic class (Dragonborn): Sorcerer

Dragons are usually extremely jealous of their personal independence, but they are so powerful they must nevertheless be counted as a major political force. Most look down upon other creatures as greatly inferior.

Culture

Dragon society is dominated by the Oath. Dragon culture regards oath-keeping as the highest morality. Anyone in possession of a dragon’s true name and able to correctly pronounce it can usually compel a dragon to abide by an oath, even to the point of suicide. Precisely because of this, dragons try to avoid being entangled in oaths and to maintain their own freedom.

Dragons generally live alone and rarely act together. Even alone, a dragon is formidable and influential; united, they would dominate the world. They have a weakness for hoarding wealth. They are also racists, regarding non-dragonkind as inferior — though they view some species more favourably than others.

The Concord

The closest thing to a political order among dragonkind is the Concord, a set of social norms and expectations. Concord dragons are expected not to attack one another without cause and to come to the aid of other Concord dragons under attack. These expectations are not formally defined as laws but are a matter of personal honour. Exactly how much effort a dragon can be expected to undertake in support of another, and what counts as “attacked,” are matters of debate.

Most dragons adhere to the Concord, but it is voluntary. Those who do not include:

  • Wild dragons — who prefer to live as pure predators, free from any kind of morality
  • Outcasts — judged by their peers to have failed to live up to expectations
  • Integrated dragons — more closely involved in the societies of lesser species, whether out of sympathy or a desire to dominate

Reproduction and Naming

Dragons are hermaphrodites. Reproduction requires two individuals, but which of the two will lay a subsequent egg only becomes apparent after the event. The egg-layer is considered the primary parent, though draconic parenting is hands-off by human standards.

Dragon names are determined by a magical ritual which occurs after mating, producing a name completely unique to that individual. Knowing a dragon’s true name is a source of power, so the information is closely guarded. Among non-dragons and even most dragons, individuals are often known by nicknames.

Dragonborn

See main article: Dragonborn

Dragons create dragonborn to serve them. Several dragonborn cities exist, ruled largely by the dragonborn themselves but always open to their allied dragons and committed to advancing their interests. Dragonborn are also sent out into the world on quests.

Three dragonborn havens are known: Dryhaven (in the Rockfire Desert), Seahaven (in the Scalewind Isles), and Icehaven (in the Icewyrm region). The dragonborn of Seahaven maintain a draconic telepathic network — a communication system that can relay messages across the continent to other dragonborn and their allied dragons.

Dragon Blood

Dragon blood is a potent magical substance, coveted for alchemical and sorcerous uses. A black market in dragon blood exists, with traffickers capturing or killing dragons to harvest it. The trade is deeply offensive to dragonkind, and any dragon who learns of it will pursue the perpetrators with extreme prejudice.

Political implications

The dragon blood trade is a matter of considerable political sensitivity. The League’s elderly Hegemon has been suffering for several years from a curse that has resisted every magical cure her court can arrange; persistent rumours — neither confirmed nor refuted — hold that she is being kept alive through the clandestine use of dragon blood. If the Concord were to confirm this, the consequences for the League would be grave. The Hegemonic Fleet is already highly vulnerable to dragon attack, and the League’s political culture is organised around not giving offence to dragonkind.

Religion

Most dragons believe themselves to be at least as old as the Old Gods. The Old Gods created the other races, but the dragons were created by no-one — they simply were. Worshipping gods is for lesser races.

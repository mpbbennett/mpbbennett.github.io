The League

Also known as: The League of Realms, the League of Solstice
Values: Justice, Authority
Symbol: Wheel, swords, and sun — white on grey
Characteristic class: Wizard

The League is an alliance of states and the largest polity in the world by population, but far more decentralised than empires like the Old Dominion or The Three.

The Compact

The League is named after the island of Solstice, a relatively insignificant territory seen as neutral ground by the major powers at the time of its formation. The founding Compact was magically sealed with an enormous ritual which gives some degree of magical compulsion to its provisions. This effect wanes over time and must be continually reinforced. Pactstones on Solstice and the other signatory states are inscribed with the charter and help maintain the magic.

Government

The Diet

The governing body of the League is the Diet, composed of representatives of each member state voting by majority rule. The League originally consisted of a small number of members but has expanded over the years. New members have the same rights as original members, except that on certain matters the Diet must achieve a majority of original members in addition to a majority of all members. Associate members have limited rights and duties and send non-voting observers.

The Diet was originally itinerant but has been located in the city of Neath for some time.

The Hegemon

One member-state serves as Hegemon, at the Diet’s pleasure. The Hegemon chairs meetings of the Diet and commands the League army and navy. The League has no standing army, although the Diet does subsidise the Hegemon to increase their personal retinue. League armies can only be raised by act of the Diet calling on member states to mobilise their own forces.

The current Hegemon is an elderly princess who has reigned for twenty-eight years and is generally respected. In the past three years she has been suffering from a degenerative affliction which has resisted all magical cures — foul play is suspected but unproven, with quiet speculation that the curse originates from the Old Dominion or from agents acting on behalf of the Forest Kingdoms. There are further rumours that she is being kept alive through the clandestine use of dragon blood, a matter that could have grave political consequences if confirmed. She has no direct heir and a succession crisis is widely anticipated.

The Hegemonic Fleet

The League does have a standing navy, known as the Hegemonic Fleet. The fleet is responsible for everyday policing of League borders and sea-lanes and maintains its own separate infrastructure and hierarchy across League states. The Grand Admiral (nominated by the Hegemon for approval of the Diet) is one of the most powerful positions in the League.

The fleet is highly vulnerable to dragon attacks. One wild dragon once burned through a squadron of 27 vessels in less than an hour. The League is therefore very careful to avoid offending the Concord.

The Basilica

The Diet originally served a judicial function, but as Compact law expanded, it created a judicial system known as the Basilica. Cases progress through various Chambers (the Red Chamber, the Purple Chamber, the Striped Chamber, the Chequered Chamber, the Ultimate Chamber, and the Chamber of the Hegemon). The Basilica’s power has expanded considerably, now hearing cases brought by individuals or corporations against League states. Given the magical nature of the Compact, Basilica judges usually have some magical training.

The League is not bound by the Compact to protect members against internal revolutions, so long as the new regime recognises obligations incurred by the previous one. Whether the Hegemon may use League forces to protect states from internal revolution is an area of considerable legal complexity.

Mage Regulation

Mages in the League and other human states are generally closely regulated by mundane rulers, mindful of the example of the Arcane Order. Mage Guilds or Wizardry Academies are tools for top-down control by rulers rather than forums for horizontal contacts between practitioners. In most cases, the ruler is formally the Rector of the Guild, the Vice-Rector is the Court Magician, and a Pro-Rector handles daily administration.

Use of magic in a territory generally requires a licence from the local guild, though active enforcement is rare. Guilds of one territory normally extend associate membership by courtesy to members of other guilds. There is no League-wide guild, although high-level wizards are well known informally to one another.

Major Cities

  • Vermillion and Azure — two rival port cities on the Isthmus, with a centuries-old hatred expressed through the famous Isthmian Games
  • Mistdam — a major commercial centre built in an earthmote zone, controlling the Wide-Awake Straits
  • Neath — seat of the Diet, a city built beneath a great shell
  • Calackra — a delta city of thirty islets at the mouth of the Bright River, the League’s chief port for the inland grain trade

The Academy

The greatest centre of magical learning in the current age is the Academy, built high in the Sunshield Mountains — a mini-city of towers in the snow, geothermally heated and connected underground. The head of the Academy is the Provost. Technically part of a duchy within the League, the Academy is effectively autonomous. Other League rulers sometimes worry, but the duchy profits from its presence.

The Guild of Disposers

The Guild of Disposers is a relatively loose association of monster-hunting adventurers. Members pay dues in return for common facilities and services. More importantly, local rulers come to the guild with offers of work, and guild members are licensed to carry weapons and use magic which might otherwise get them in trouble with the law.

Timeline

The League is roughly 200 years old, comparable to the Holy Roman Empire from the emergence of its electoral system to its heyday.

Calackra

Calackra is a League city built across a great river delta, where the Bright River empties into the sea through some thirty inhabited islets and a thicket of lesser channels. It is the chief inland port of the League’s southern coast: ocean-going vessels tranship their cargoes to shallow-draft barges at Calackra for the long journey upriver, and the return traffic of grain, wool, and salted river-fish passes back through the same wharves.

For all practical purposes the city has been governed for the past eighty-three years by the executors of a will whose author has not yet died.

The Sleeping Duke

Calackra was a duchy until 20AC, ruled by Grand Duke Lodewijk II van Calackra, the last of the old line. Lodewijk’s testament, drafted in his thirty-ninth year, was famously elaborate — twelve named executors, sixty-odd specific bequests, and provisions for the contingencies of his minority heirs. Among its standard clauses was a recitation of settled League doctrine on testamentary regency: in the event of the testator’s incapacity, his named executors were to administer the duchy in trust until his recovery or death.

In the spring of 20AC the Duke fell into a sleep from which neither physicians nor Wayfinders could rouse him. The cause has never been formally established. He has not woken in the eighty-three years since, nor has he aged, nor has his hair or fingernails grown beyond an early, fixed length. He is alive. The Executors took up the regency as the will required, and have held it in unbroken sequence to the present day.

Petitions for a finding of death have been refused four times by the Basilica, most recently in 89AC. The Court’s standing position is that an unageing body kept whole by an active enchantment is incapacitated, not deceased, and that the Duke’s heirs accordingly have no claim. Each ruling has been signed under the Compact and is, by the Compact’s own reflexive provision, magically binding on every member state. Lawyers refer to the doctrine as Calackran rest; it is the most-taught case in Basilica curricula after the Compact itself.

Government

The Executors number twenty-nine — the original twelve and seventeen co-opted seats added over generations as the duchy’s revenues outgrew the original administrative compass. When a seat falls vacant the remaining Executors elect a replacement, by custom from among the city’s chartered merchant houses: the Voorlinden, the van Esterling, the Bekkerwijk, the Heerings of Saltquay, the Vandermeer dyestuffs concern, and a dozen lesser. The body is therefore a co-optive merchant oligarchy in fact, while remaining in form a panel of trustees administering a private estate. Calackra has no duke, no count, no chartered burgesses, and no mayor; its merchants govern themselves under cover of a will in probate.

The Executors’ chamber, the Stillhall, occupies the great hall of the old ducal palace. The council table is the Duke’s sarcophagus: a slab of clear leaded glass set in a frame of black slate and silver, beneath which Lodewijk lies in court dress, breathing twice a minute. The Executors deliberate over him. By a custom older than any of the present incumbents, no measure may be voted on until it has been read aloud at the head of the table, where the Duke’s ear lies.

The public has a right of audience between the third and the fifth bell each weekday, established by Basilica injunction in 41AC after a riot over the Duke’s reported death. Visitors file past the glass in silence. Lodewijk is by some distance the most-visited person in the League.

Twice a year the Duke speaks in his sleep. The words are recorded by the Chamberlain of the Watch and lodged with the Basilica. They are usually fragmentary and have never been treated as binding instruction, though one phrase — let the salt go free — is widely held to have inspired the suspension of internal salt-tolls in 67AC. The diaries of the Chamberlain are sealed for forty years after each entry and are among the most contested documents in the city’s archives.

The Five Burdens

The Sleep is not self-sustaining. Five Executors at any time hold the office of Keeper, each charged with one component of the maintaining spellwork: the breath, the warmth, the slowing of the blood, the integrity of the body, and the silver thread that ties the soul. The originals were laid by a Kin necromancer-for-hire whose name has been struck from the public record; the techniques are taught on initiation and nowhere else. A Keeper who fails his charge forfeits his seat and, by ordinance of 38AC, his property.

The arrangement is the open secret of the city. Calackrans speak of “the Five Burdens” the way other cities speak of their gates or walls. It is the standing joke of the wharf-songs that the Duke is the hardest-working man in the League, in that without his sleep the salt would not move.

Districts

The inner islets hold Saltquay (the warehouses and panning flats), the Tapmarket (the brewers’ quarter, where a local rice-beer called kelder is made), and Owlbridge (the old merchants’ district, named for a carved lintel above its main span). Further out, Ironferry handles smithing and the small-boat yards, and Reedwick is a neighbourhood of fishermen and thatchers living largely over the water on pile-driven platforms. The outermost ring, the Outer Wards, is a low-rent sprawl of incomers, seasonal labour, and the overflow of the barge-crews between sailings.

Every islet is connected to its neighbours by bridge or ferry, and right of passage on the main canals is a point of chronic dispute. Ferrymen are licensed by ward, not by city, so a route that crosses three wards requires three licences and three sets of tolls. The Canalwatch, the Executors’ standing watch, polices the channels above the low-water mark; below it, jurisdiction is famously unclear, and the Kin have made profitable use of the gap for generations.

Trade

Calackra’s principal exports are salt, freshwater pearls from the upper delta, and river-fish preserved in a local mustard brine that keeps six months in a sealed crock. It is the League’s chief port for the grain trade out of the interior plains, and its granaries are audited four times a year by a Basilica inspector — a rare instance of the Basilica intervening directly in a city’s commerce. The Combine maintains a counting-house here, resented by the local guilds and barred by standing ordinance from owning barges outright. The Kin are active among the bargewrights, where they run a quiet protection trade under a very thin fig leaf — and where, as the city is occasionally reminded, they hold a hereditary professional interest in the Stillhall’s continued operation.

The Heirs

The Duke’s collateral heirs — House Doorne and House Sleiden — maintain a residence in Vermillion and Azure and a smaller establishment in Mistdam. They petition the Basilica every few decades. They have never won. The current head of House Doorne is in her seventies and is widely understood to have given up the suit in fact, though not in form.

Periodically — most recently in 92AC — agents claiming to act for the heirs have attempted to introduce one or another of the rumoured rousing-spells into the Stillhall. None has so far succeeded. The Canalwatch and the Keepers between them are vigilant. The 92AC attempt produced the only execution in the city’s recent record; the rope was tied by an Executor.

Culture

The city’s houses are roofed in black slate quarried upstream, which gives the skyline a charcoal cast even on bright days. Bridge-building is the civic art: Calackrans compete for the honour of sponsoring a new span, and the oldest bridge, the Owlbridge itself, carries carved heads of its sponsors stacked generations deep along its balustrade. The local oath is sworn “on the turning tide”, a reference to the river’s tidal reach. The traditional civic dish is hearthbraise, a slow-cooked stew of river-eel, onion, and a sweet black wheat.

Calackra celebrates Salt Week each spring — seven days during which the pans are drained, cleaned, and reconsecrated, and all internal tolls are suspended. The gesture of municipal generosity doubles as the single biggest trading week of the Calackran year. On the first day of Salt Week the Duke is shaved.

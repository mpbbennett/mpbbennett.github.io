Dragonborn

Dragonborn are individuals who have devoted their lives to serving a dragon and have been transformed by that service, gaining draconic features and longer lifespans. Most are not born dragonborn — the bond is taken on in early adulthood and does not pass to one’s children. A dragonborn’s patron claims first call on their time, and a dragonborn seen abroad is almost always travelling on dragon business.

The Bond

The transformation is not a single rite but a gradual process taking several years. An initiate takes up residence near their chosen dragon, performs services on the patron’s behalf, and is slowly altered. Scales and horn-buds appear first; the matching breath weapon — fire, cold, acid, lightning, or poison — manifests last, and marks the bond as complete. An initiate can withdraw before the breath weapon comes, at which point the incipient changes reverse within a season. After it, the bond is permanent, and the dragonborn will age with their patron rather than against them.

Every completed dragonborn wears a small token from their patron’s hoard — usually a coin or a knuckle-bone — given at the close of the bond and worn openly. Stealing a dragonborn’s token is an extreme offence, pursued by the patron dragon personally.

Dragonborn Havens

Three havens exist, each built around a concentration of dragons willing to accept dragonborn. All three are ruled by councils of senior dragonborn drawn from the different patron-lines, who mediate between their dragons and administer the common business of the city. The patron dragons themselves rarely attend council.

Dryhaven (Rockfire Desert)

The largest haven, built around a spring at the northern edge of the Rockfire Desert. Patron dragons here are predominantly red and brass. The city is carved into a rose-coloured sandstone bluff, with the older quarters underground where the heat is tolerable. Dryhaven’s dragonborn function as the havens’ diplomats — desert caravan goods pass through on Sand Halfling routes, and the haven’s council takes a percentage in exchange for guarding the routes against the wilder desert fauna. Most formal correspondence between the havens is written in Dryhaven.

Seahaven (Scalewind Isles)

Built on the south coast of Great Scalewind, where a line of low sea-cliffs allows dragon lairs above the city and dragonborn quarters below. Seahaven is the only haven where dragons are seen in the streets; its older patrons predate the haven itself and regard the settlement as an extension of their lairs. Seahaven is also the anchor of the draconic telepathic network — a relay system by which short messages pass along a chain of dragonborn, each standing a relay watch of a few hours at a time. Continental transmission typically takes a day or two. Network technicians are trained at Seahaven and seconded to the other havens on rotation. The Silver Dwarves of the same islands hold Seahaven in contempt, and trade between the haven and the dwarven delves is routed through neutral human villages on the north coast.

Icehaven (Icewyrm)

The smallest and most austere, built into a glacial valley in the Icewyrm region, within distant reach of Sapphirehold but not in contact with it. Patron dragons here are almost all white, and Icehaven dragonborn take on pale, frost-tolerant features more pronounced than at the other havens. The local economy rests on ice fishing and the collection of coldglass, a crystal formed where a dragon’s cold-breath has struck seawater, which is shipped to the other havens and to the League for alchemical use. Icehaven maintains a standing militia — Frost Giant raiders from across the southern seas regard the haven as a defensible target, and the patron dragons cannot always be relied upon to be present.

Quests and service

Dragonborn are frequently sent abroad on their patron’s business — retrieving an item for the hoard, or standing as a long-term agent in a foreign city. A dragonborn travelling alone is presumed to be on such an errand, and most settlements offer passage and lodging without asking the purpose, as the alternative is to offend the patron. Postings in League cities can last decades. A dragonborn in residence in Mistdam or Neath is usually the eyes and ears of a dragon who wishes to know what is said of them there.

The Dragon Kingdom

One dragon and her dragonborn conquered a human kingdom and set themselves up as a despotic ruling caste. Other dragons look down on her actions. As the dragon grows old and weak, the dragonborn debate whether one of their own should succeed her or whether they should seek another dragon ruler.

Language

Dragonborn speak Draconic in a clipped manner. The verbose registers of full Draconic are considered the prerogative of dragons, and dragonborn abbreviate to avoid presumption. Every dragonborn’s given name incorporates some form of their patron dragon’s name, with conventions varying by haven. Dryhaven uses a patronymic suffix derived from the dragon’s true name. Seahaven prefixes a syllable drawn from the dragon’s nickname. Icehaven inserts the patron’s initial as a second given name.

Relations with Others

The Silver Dwarves of the Scalewind Isles have a healthy competitive respect for dragons but hold the dragonborn in contempt, viewing their devotion as slavish. Elsewhere the ancestry is regarded with wary courtesy: a dragonborn’s patron is rarely far in influence even if far in distance, and a slight against a dragonborn can become a slight against a dragon.

Idioms

A collection of cultural idioms and sayings from across Moonrise.

Common Idioms

  • “Hungry enough to eat the north end of a south-bound stallion” — very hungry
  • “Stinks enough to gag a goblin”
  • “Haven’t got the sense to tell a bishop from a bugbear”
  • “Hot as a goblin ass in a pepper patch”
  • “Can’t see the bottom of an empty mug” — drunk
  • “Like pissin’ in a wicker basket” — impossible or useless
  • “Kenku see, kenku do” — one who imitates without understanding
  • “By the Sun/Void!” — interjection of extreme surprise
  • “Grab the dwarf by the beard” — start a fight for no reason
  • “Crusties” — undead (especially Charophage-afflicted)
  • “Rusties” — Warforged
  • “Could wilt the ears off an elf” — very strong liquor
  • “You ought to hold your sword by the hilt” — keep it simple
  • “Even squirrels can fall from trees” — even experts are wrong

Dragonborn Idioms

  • “A long winter makes a sweet wine” — endure hardship for later rewards
  • “Look before you sneeze” — be cautious
  • “Have scales of many colours” — very open-minded
  • “Dragons inviting a sheep to dinner” — taking advantage of someone

Drow Idioms

  • “To give spiderblood” — spend too much on a gift
  • “Knowledge from deceit” — motto
  • “Light upon you!” — curse
  • “Melt the bones” — destroy completely, leave no evidence
  • “When Lolth giggles” — something so funny the goddess would laugh
  • “Tired but never weak” — do not show weakness

Dwarven Idioms

  • “Fire in my beard!” — interjection of surprise
  • “By Hamrongeid’s hammer!” — interjection of surprise
  • “Swinging true” — sounds right
  • “No problem a hammer can’t fix!”
  • “Clay-headed/mud-headed” — ignorant, naive, or stupid
  • “Sharp as a fresh pick” — clever or efficient
  • “Like folding stone” — impossible
  • “So long as Aevilog burns bright” — forever
  • “Pissing steam” — very angry
  • “Landslides don’t ask directions” — be direct

Elven Idioms

  • “Hunter’s blood!” — vulgar interjection
  • “On wilted ears” — hard of hearing, naive
  • “Giving the dragon’s share” — being stingy
  • “Poor rooting (cirtlag)” — extremely low-born
  • “Ni linn” — neutral term for non-elven peoples
  • “May you outlast your sword!” — caution for the overconfident
  • “Arrows in the wind” — subject to the whims of fate

Halfling Idioms

  • “Quit braiding my ears!” — quit teasing me
  • “Shiny” — something amazing you like or want
  • “Hair on all sides” — generally unpleasant situation
  • “Get your eyes stuck on X” — become obsessed
  • “Gold for the tinsmith” — something too fancy
  • “Thinking in knots” — confused
  • “Longer than a gnome wedding” — very lengthy affair
  • “Go sell it to the Lyres” — tell unbelievable stories to a bard

Seafaring Idioms

  • “Who seeds winds breeds storms” — significant deeds have bigger consequences
  • “To sail with all winds” — drift, follow others with no goal
  • “Swimming with the fish” — died at sea
  • “Saltblooded” — old experienced sailors
  • “Two captains, three opinions” — no good from ambiguous leadership
  • “Banking on low tide” — make a final stand
